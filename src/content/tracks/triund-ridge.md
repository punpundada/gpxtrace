## How the Track Feels Underfoot

The first two kilometers out of Gallu Devi temple lull you into thinking this is a gentle Himalayan balcony walk. The grade hovers around 11–14% over packed earth and pine needles, shaded by oak and rhododendron canopy. Keep your cadence conservative here—the real vertical work waits around the third spur.

Once the GPX trace bends east into the **22 Curves**, the stone steps steepen sharply. You gain nearly 500 vertical meters in less than two kilometers. Because the risers are uneven granite blocks, trekking poles save significant quad fatigue on both the push up and the return descent.

## Crossing the Triund Saddle to Snowline

Most day hikers stop at the grassy Triund ridge (`2,842 m`), where the trail briefly levels out. Continuing northeast toward the Laka glacier moraine transforms the character of the route:

- **Kilometer 3.8 to 4.9**: Exposed alpine meadow traversing above a steep drop into the Bhagsu gorge.
- **Kilometer 4.9 to Finish**: Loose slate and frost-shattered boulders where route-finding in afternoon mist relies heavily on having the offline GPX trace loaded.

## Field Verdict

Start before 06:00 to beat both the mule trains on the lower steps and the convective cloud buildup that typically rolls over the ridge by 11:30. As a half-day vertical workout with genuine big-mountain payoff, few tracks in the region match its efficiency.
